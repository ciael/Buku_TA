\noindent\textbf{Abstract}

% Ubah paragraf berikut dengan abstrak dari tugas akhir dalam Bahasa Inggris
\emph{Water pressure in household pumps tends to be unstable, especially when
several faucets are opened simultaneously, so a system capable of maintaining
stable water pressure is required. Common
solutions rely on additional external devices such as a pressure switch acting
on the pump output side. This study proposes a different approach, namely
controlling the pressure from the power-supply side through a PWM AC chopper
based on a diode bridge and two IGBTs with a neural-network-based controller. The controller is a 3-4-1
Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) whose inputs are the pressure error, the change of
error, and the duty cycle, and whose output is the duty-cycle increment; it is
trained from closed-loop operating data (behavior cloning). The test results show that the
PWM AC chopper regulates the output RMS
voltage linearly with the duty cycle, with less than 1\% deviation from the
theoretical value, and a measured dead time of 1.48--1.49~$\mu$s as designed.
The neural network model achieves a coefficient of determination $R^2 = 0.915$
on the test set, indicating good accuracy in predicting the control action. In system testing, the controller maintains the pressure at the setpoint with a
steady-state error $\leq 0.009$~bar for one to four open faucets. Setpoint
tracking tests (0.25--0.35~bar) show settling times of 4--7~s, while load
disturbance rejection tests recover the pressure within 9--15~s. A physical
limitation occurs when all faucets are closed or when four faucets are opened
simultaneously, causing the pump's minimum output pressure to exceed the setpoint
and drive the actuator into saturation. Hence, the supply-side
neural-network-based water pump pressure control system is shown to operate against load variations.}
